## 2. Proof of the Push-Relabel Key Lemma

> **Key Lemma:** Let $f$ be a preflow in $G = (V, E)$. If $v \in V$ has positive excess $e_f(v) > 0$, then there is a directed path from $v$ to $s$ in the residual network $G_f$.

**Proof (via Reachability Partition on Positive-Flow Edges):**
1. Define $A = \{w \in V : \text{there is a directed path } s \leadsto w \text{ in } G \text{ using only edges with } f_e > 0\}$, and let $B = V \setminus A$. Note that $s \in A$, so $s \notin B$.
2. For every edge $e = (x, y)$ on an $s \leadsto w$ path with $f_e > 0$, the reverse edge $(y, x)$ exists in $G_f$ with residual capacity $f_e > 0$. Thus **every $w \in A$ has a directed path $w \leadsto s$ in $G_f$**.
3. It remains to show that **no vertex in $B$ can have positive excess** (so every active vertex $v$ belongs to $A$).
   - By definition of $A$, no edge $e = (x, y)$ with $x \in A, y \in B$ can have $f_e > 0$ (otherwise $y$ would be reachable from $s$ and thus in $A$). Hence $\sum_{e \in \delta^-(B)} f_e = 0$.
   - Summing excess over all $w \in B$ (and canceling internal edges within $B$):
     $$\sum_{w \in B} e_f(w) = \sum_{e \in \delta^-(B)} f_e - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(B)} f_e = 0 - \sum_{e \in \delta^+(B)} f_e \le 0$$
   - Since $f$ is a preflow and $s \notin B$, every term $e_f(w) \ge 0$ for $w \in B$. A sum of non-negative numbers is $\le 0$ iff **every single term is zero**: $e_f(w) = 0$ for all $w \in B$. $\blacksquare$

## 3. Application 1: Image Segmentation via Minimum Cut

### 3.1 Problem Formulation
Given an undirected pixel adjacency graph $G = (V, E)$ (e.g., a 2D grid of pixels where edges connect neighboring pixels):
- Each pixel $v \in V$ has:
  - $a_v \ge 0$: likelihood score of belonging to the **foreground**.
  - $b_v \ge 0$: likelihood score of belonging to the **background**.
- Each pair of neighboring pixels $e = (u, v) \in E$ has a **separation penalty** $p_e \ge 0$ incurred if one pixel is placed in the foreground and the other in the background (encouraging spatial smoothness/contiguous regions).

**Goal:** Partition pixels $V$ into Foreground $X$ and Background $Y = V \setminus X$ to maximize net score:
$$\max_{(X, Y)} \left( \sum_{v \in X} a_v + \sum_{v \in Y} b_v - \sum_{e \in E \text{ crossing } (X, Y)} p_e \right)$$

### 3.2 Converting Maximization to Minimization
Let $W = \sum_{v \in V} (a_v + b_v)$ be a fixed constant. Since $\sum_{v \in X} a_v + \sum_{v \in Y} b_v = W - \sum_{v \in Y} a_v - \sum_{v \in X} b_v$, maximizing the objective is equivalent to **minimizing the penalty of missed likelihoods plus boundary penalties**:
$$\min_{(X, Y)} \left( \sum_{v \in Y} a_v + \sum_{v \in X} b_v + \sum_{e \in E \text{ crossing } (X, Y)} p_e \right)$$

### 3.3 Reduction to $(s,t)$-Minimum Cut
Construct a directed flow network $G' = (V \cup \{s, t\}, E')$ as follows:
1. Replace each undirected neighbor edge $e = \{u, v\} \in E$ with **two directed arcs** $(u, v)$ and $(v, u)$, each with capacity $p_e$.
2. Add source $s$ (representing Foreground) and directed edges $(s, v)$ with capacity $a_v$ for all $v \in V$.
3. Add sink $t$ (representing Background) and directed edges $(v, t)$ with capacity $b_v$ for all $v \in V$.

```text
                    Foreground Source (s)
                      /       |       \
               cap a_u      cap a_v   cap a_w
                    ↙         ↓         ↘
                 (u) <==p_e==> (v) <==p_e==> (w)    [Pixel Grid]
                    ↘         ↓         ↙
               cap b_u      cap b_v   cap b_w
                      \       |       /
                    Background Sink (t)
```

**One-to-One Cut Correspondence:**
For any $(s,t)$-cut $(A, B)$ in $G'$, let $X = A \setminus \{s\}$ (Foreground) and $Y = B \setminus \{t\}$ (Background). The directed edges leaving $A$ and entering $B$ are:
- $(s, v)$ for $v \in Y$ $\implies$ contributes $\sum_{v \in Y} a_v$.
- $(v, t)$ for $v \in X$ $\implies$ contributes $\sum_{v \in X} b_v$.
- Exactly one directed arc $(u, v)$ (where $u \in X, v \in Y$) for each undirected edge $\{u, v\}$ crossing $(X, Y)$ $\implies$ contributes $\sum_{e \text{ crossing } (X,Y)} p_e$.
Thus $c(A, B)$ equals the exact minimization objective!

## 4. Application 2: Maximum Cardinality Bipartite Matching

### 4.1 Problem Definition
Given an undirected **bipartite graph** $G = (V \cup W, E)$ where all edges have one endpoint in $V$ and one endpoint in $W$, find a **matching** $M \subseteq E$ (a set of vertex-disjoint edges, i.e., no two edges share an endpoint) of maximum cardinality $|M|$.

### 4.2 Reduction to Maximum Flow
Construct a directed flow network $G' = (\{s, t\} \cup V \cup W, E')$:
1. Add source $s$ with directed edges $(s, v)$ of capacity $1$ for every $v \in V$.
2. Direct every original edge $e = (v, w) \in E$ from $V$ to $W$ with capacity $1$ (or $\infty$).
3. Add sink $t$ with directed edges $(w, t)$ of capacity $1$ for every $w \in W$.

```text
               Left Side V            Right Side W
                  (v_1) ----[1/∞]----> (w_1)
         [1]    ↗       \            ↗       \    [1]
     (s) -----> (v_2) ---\--[1/∞]---/--> (w_2) -----> (t)
         [1]    ↘         ↘        /         ↗    [1]
                  (v_3) ----[1/∞]----> (w_3)
```

### 4.3 Correctness & Integrality Theorem
> **Theorem:** The maximum cardinality of a matching in $G$ equals the value of the maximum $(s,t)$-flow in $G'$. Moreover, any integer-valued maximum flow $f^*$ (guaranteed to exist by the Integrality Property of Ford-Fulkerson) defines a maximum matching $M = \{e \in E : f^*_e = 1\}$ of size $\text{val}(f^*)$.

**Proof:**
- **Matching $\implies$ Flow:** Given matching $M$, send $1$ unit of flow along $s \to v \to w \to t$ for each $(v, w) \in M$. Since $M$ is vertex-disjoint, every $(s, v)$ and $(w, t)$ edge carries $\le 1$ unit of flow, yielding an integer flow of value $|M|$.
- **Integral Flow $\implies$ Matching:** Given an integer flow $f$, each $v \in V$ receives at most $1$ unit from $s$, so by conservation at most one outgoing edge $(v, w)$ has $f_{vw} = 1$. Symmetrically, each $w \in W$ can send at most $1$ unit to $t$, so at most one incoming edge $(v, w)$ has $f_{vw} = 1$. Thus $M = \{(v, w) \in E : f_{vw} = 1\}$ is a valid matching of size $\text{val}(f)$. $\blacksquare$

## 5. Hall's Marriage Theorem via Max-Flow Min-Cut

### 5.1 Statement of Hall's Theorem
For any subset $S \subseteq V$, let $\Gamma(S) = \{w \in W : \exists v \in S \text{ with } (v, w) \in E\}$ denote the neighborhood of $S$.

> **Theorem (Hall's Marriage Theorem):** A bipartite graph $G = (V \cup W, E)$ has a matching that saturates every vertex in $V$ (i.e., a matching of size $|V|$) **if and only if** Hall's Condition holds:
> $$|\Gamma(S)| \ge |S| \quad \text{for all } S \subseteq V$$

### 5.2 Proof Using the Max-Flow Min-Cut Theorem
- **Necessity ($\implies$):** Trivial—if every vertex in $S$ is matched to a distinct neighbor in $\Gamma(S)$, then $|\Gamma(S)| \ge |S|$.
- **Sufficiency ($\Longleftarrow$):** Set middle edge capacities $u_{vw} = \infty$ for all $(v, w) \in E$ (and $u_{sv} = 1, u_{wt} = 1$). By the Max-Flow Min-Cut Theorem, $G$ has a matching of size $|V|$ iff every $(s,t)$-cut $(A, B)$ in $G'$ has capacity $c(A, B) \ge |V|$.
  - Let $(A, B)$ be any $(s,t)$-cut in $G'$, and let $S = V \cap A$ be the left-side vertices on the source side of the cut.
  - **Case 1:** Some edge $(v, w) \in E$ has $v \in S$ and $w \in W \cap B$. Then $(v, w)$ crosses the cut and has capacity $\infty$, so $c(A, B) = \infty \ge |V|$.
  - **Case 2:** Every neighbor of $S$ lies inside $A$, meaning $\Gamma(S) \subseteq W \cap A$. Then:
    - For every $v \in V \setminus S$, the edge $(s, v)$ crosses from $s \in A$ to $v \in B$ (contributing $|V \setminus S| = |V| - |S|$).
    - For every $w \in \Gamma(S) \subseteq A$, the edge $(w, t)$ crosses from $w \in A$ to $t \in B$ (contributing at least $|\Gamma(S)|$).
    - Therefore, by Hall's Condition ($|\Gamma(S)| \ge |S|$):
      $$c(A, B) \ge (|V| - |S|) + |\Gamma(S)| \ge |V| - |S| + |S| = |V| \quad \blacksquare$$
