## 1. Executive Summary

This final lecture of CS261 presents one of the crowning achievements of modern approximation algorithms—the **Goemans-Williamson (1994) $0.87856$-Approximation Algorithm for Maximum Cut via Semidefinite Programming (SDP)**—followed by a synthesis of the **Top 10 Ideas of CS261**:
1. **The Maximum Cut Problem & The $\frac{1}{2}$ Barrier:** Given an undirected graph $G = (V, E)$ with edge weights $w_{ij} \ge 0$, partition $V$ into $(A, B)$ maximizing $\sum_{i \in A, j \in B} w_{ij}$. While random cuts, greedy, local search, and standard LP relaxations all get stuck at $\frac{1}{2} = 0.5$, Goemans and Williamson broke through to $\alpha_{\text{GW}} \approx 0.87856$!
2. **Exact Quadratic Formulation $\to$ Vector Program Relaxation:** Expressing Max-Cut exactly over $y_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ as $\max \frac{1}{2} \sum_{(i,j)\in E} w_{ij}(1 - y_i y_j)$, and relaxing 1D scalars $y_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ (the 0-sphere $S^0$) to **$n$-dimensional unit vectors $x_i \in S^{n-1} \subset \mathbb{R}^n$** ($\|x_i\|_2 = 1$).
3. **Equivalence to Semidefinite Programming (SDP) & Polynomial-Time Solvability:** Why optimizing a linear function of inner products $P_{ij} = x_i \cdot x_j$ is equivalent to a Linear Program over **Positive Semidefinite (PSD) matrices $P \succeq 0$**, solvable in polynomial time via the **Ellipsoid Method** (using an eigenvalue/eigenvector separation oracle) or **Interior Point Methods**.
4. **Random Hyperplane Rounding:** Slicing the unit sphere $S^{n-1}$ with a uniformly random hyperplane through the origin separates vectors $x_i, x_j$ at angle $\theta_{ij}$ with probability $\frac{\theta_{ij}}{\pi}$, yielding approximation ratio:
   $$\alpha_{\text{GW}} = \min_{\theta \in (0, \pi]} \frac{\theta / \pi}{(1 - \cos\theta)/2} \approx \mathbf{0.87856}$$

## 2. From Exact Quadratic Program to Vector Program & SDP

### 2.1 Step 1: Exact Quadratic Program over $\{-1, +1\}^n$
Assign each vertex $i \in V$ a spin variable $y_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ ($y_i = +1 \iff i \in A$, $y_i = -1 \iff i \in B$). Notice that:
$$\frac{1 - y_i y_j}{2} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } y_i \ne y_j \quad (\text{edge } (i, j) \text{ is cut!}) \\ 0 & \text{if } y_i = y_j \quad (\text{edge } (i, j) \text{ is not cut}) \end{cases}$$
Thus Max-Cut is **equivalent** to the (NP-hard) quadratic program:
$$\max_{y \in \{-1, +1\}^n} \quad \frac{1}{2} \sum_{(i, j) \in E} w_{ij} (1 - y_i y_j)$$

### 2.2 Step 2: The Goemans-Williamson Vector Program Relaxation
Notice that $\{-1, +1\}$ is simply the set of **unit vectors in $\mathbb{R}^1$**. Goemans and Williamson relaxed each $y_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ to a **unit vector $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^n$ on the unit sphere $S^{n-1}$** ($\|x_i\|_2 = \sqrt{x_i \cdot x_i} = 1$), replacing the scalar product $y_i y_j$ with the dot product $x_i \cdot x_j$:
$$\text{(Vector Program)} \qquad \max_{x_1, \dots, x_n \in \mathbb{R}^n} \quad \frac{1}{2} \sum_{(i, j) \in E} w_{ij} (1 - x_i \cdot x_j) \qquad \text{s.t.} \quad x_i \cdot x_i = 1 \quad \forall i = 1, \dots, n$$
*(Geometrically, for each edge $(i, j) \in E$, the objective $\frac{1}{2}(1 - \cos\theta_{ij})$ wants to push $x_i$ and $x_j$ to be as **antipodal** ($\theta_{ij} \approx \pi, \; x_i \cdot x_j \approx -1$) on the sphere as possible!)*

### 2.3 Step 3: Equivalence to Semidefinite Programming (SDP) & PSD Matrices
How can we solve the non-linear Vector Program in polynomial time? Introduce scalar decision variables $P_{ij} = x_i \cdot x_j$ forming a symmetric $n \times n$ **Gram matrix** $P \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$.

> **Fundamental Theorem of Positive Semidefinite (PSD) Matrices (TFAE):** For a symmetric matrix $P \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$, the following four conditions are equivalent:
> 1. **Gram Matrix Factorization:** $P = X^\top X$ for some matrix $X = [x_1 \cdots x_n] \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ (i.e., $P_{ij} = x_i \cdot x_j$ for all $i, j$; recoverable from $P$ via Cholesky / spectral factorization).
> 2. **Non-Negative Quadratic Form:** $z^\top P z = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n z_i z_j P_{ij} \ge 0$ for **all** vectors $z \in \mathbb{R}^n$.
> 3. **Non-Negative Eigenvalues:** All eigenvalues $\lambda_1(P), \dots, \lambda_n(P)$ of $P$ are $\ge 0$.

Using characterization (2), the Vector Program is **identical** to an infinite-constraint Linear Program (an **SDP**) over variables $\{P_{ij}\}$:
$$\begin{aligned}
\max_{P \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}} \quad & \frac{1}{2} \sum_{(i, j) \in E} w_{ij} (1 - P_{ij}) \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & P_{ii} = 1 \qquad \forall i = 1, \dots, n \\
& P_{ij} = P_{ji} \qquad \forall i, j \\
& \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n (z_i z_j) P_{ij} \ge 0 \qquad \forall z \in \mathbb{R}^n \quad (\text{i.e., } P \succeq 0)
\end{aligned}$$
- **Why SDP Is Polynomial-Time Solvable (Separation Oracle for Ellipsoid):** Given candidate symmetric matrix $P$, compute its minimum eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}$ and unit eigenvector $z^*$ in $O(n^3)$ time. If $\lambda_{\min} \ge 0$, $P$ is PSD! If $\lambda_{\min} < 0$, then $(z^*)^\top P z^* = \lambda_{\min} < 0$, so the linear inequality $\sum_{i,j} (z^*_i z^*_j) P_{ij} \ge 0$ is **violated** and returned by the Separation Oracle! *(In practice, SDPs are solved directly via Interior Point Methods.)*

## 3. Goemans-Williamson Random Hyperplane Rounding ($0.87856$-Approximation)

### 3.1 The Algorithm
Once the SDP is solved to obtain optimal unit vectors $x_1^*, \dots, x_n^* \in S^{n-1}$:

```text
GOEMANS-WILLIAMSON-MAX-CUT(Graph G = (V, E), weights w):
    1. Solve the Max-Cut SDP to obtain optimal unit vectors x_1*, ..., x_n* in S^{n-1}
    2. Sample a uniformly random unit normal vector r in S^{n-1}
       (by drawing r_1, ..., r_n ~ N(0, 1) i.i.d. and normalizing r <- r / ||r||_2)
    3. Slice the sphere with hyperplane {z : r . z = 0}:
           A = {i in V : r . x_i* >= 0},    B = {i in V : r . x_i* < 0}
    return Cut (A, B)
```

```text
                       Random Hyperplane {z : r . z = 0}
                                   \
                          * x_i*    \       Side A (r . x >= 0)
                           \         \
                            \  θ_ij   \
                             O---------\--------
                            /           \
                           /             \  Side B (r . x < 0)
                          * x_j*          \
       (Probability that random hyperplane separates x_i* and x_j* is exactly θ_ij / π!)
```

### 3.2 Geometric Probability & Trigonometric Analysis
Fix any edge $(i, j) \in E$, and project onto the 2D plane spanned by $x_i^*$ and $x_j^*$. Let $\theta_{ij} = \arccos(x_i^* \cdot x_j^*) \in [0, \pi]$ be the angle between $x_i^*$ and $x_j^*$:
1. **Exact Probability Edge $(i, j)$ Is Cut:** In the 2D plane spanned by $x_i^*$ and $x_j^*$, the random hyperplane intersects the plane in a uniformly random line through the origin. This line separates $x_i^*$ and $x_j^*$ iff its normal falls into the two angular wedges of angle $\theta_{ij}$ out of $2\pi$ (or $\theta_{ij}$ out of $\pi$):
   $$\Pr\big[(i, j) \text{ is cut by } (A, B)\big] = \frac{\theta_{ij}}{\pi}$$
2. **Comparing Expected Cut Weight to the SDP Objective via Linearity of Expectation:**
   - Edge $(i, j)$ contributes $w_{ij} \left(\frac{\theta_{ij}}{\pi}\right)$ to $\mathbb{E}[\text{Weight}(A, B)]$.
   - Edge $(i, j)$ contributed $w_{ij} \left(\frac{1 - x_i^* \cdot x_j^*}{2}\right) = w_{ij} \left(\frac{1 - \cos\theta_{ij}}{2}\right)$ to $\text{OPT}_{\text{SDP}}$.
   - Calculus shows that for **all** angles $\theta \in (0, \pi]$:
     $$\frac{\theta / \pi}{(1 - \cos\theta)/2} \ge \alpha_{\text{GW}} \approx 0.87856 \quad \text{(attained at } \theta \approx 133.6^\circ\text{)}$$
   - Therefore, by **Linearity of Expectation**:
     $$\mathbb{E}[\text{Weight}(A, B)] = \sum_{(i, j) \in E} w_{ij} \left(\frac{\theta_{ij}}{\pi}\right) \ge \alpha_{\text{GW}} \sum_{(i, j) \in E} w_{ij} \left(\frac{1 - \cos\theta_{ij}}{2}\right) = 0.87856 \cdot \text{OPT}_{\text{SDP}} \ge \mathbf{0.87856 \cdot \text{OPT}} \quad \blacksquare$$

*(Historical Note: Under Khot's **Unique Games Conjecture (2002)**, $0.87856\dots$ is the exact threshold below which Max-Cut is in $\mathbf{P}$ and above which it is NP-hard!)*
