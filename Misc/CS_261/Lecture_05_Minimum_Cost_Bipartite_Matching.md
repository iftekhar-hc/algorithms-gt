## 1. Executive Summary

Having solved **Maximum Cardinality Bipartite Matching** via reduction to Max-Flow in Lecture 4, this lecture introduces **Minimum-Cost Perfect Bipartite Matching** (also known as the **Assignment Problem**):
- **Why Max-Flow Alone Is Not Enough:** Capacities restrict *feasibility*, whereas edge costs $c_e$ express *preferences* over feasible matchings.
- **Structural Optimality Condition:** A perfect matching $M$ is minimum-cost **if and only if** there is no **negative-cost $M$-alternating cycle**.
- **Algorithmic Compilation (Vertex Prices & Reduced Costs):** Attach a price $p_v \in \mathbb{R}$ to each vertex $v \in V \cup W$, define reduced cost $c_e^p = c_{vw} - p_v - p_w$, and maintain two invariants (**Non-negative reduced costs** $c_e^p \ge 0$ for all $e$, and **Tight matched edges** $c_e^p = 0$ for all $e \in M$).
- **The Hungarian Algorithm (Jacobi ~1840s; König, Egerváry, Kuhn 1955, Munkres 1957):** Starts with $M = \emptyset, p = 0$ and alternates between **augmenting $M$ along a tight alternating path** and **updating prices on a constricted tight set** until $|M| = n$.

## 2. Problem Formulation & WLOG Assumptions

### 2.1 Input and Objective
Given an undirected bipartite graph $G = (V \cup W, E)$ with edge costs $c_e \in \mathbb{R}$ for $e \in E$:
- **Goal:** Find a **perfect matching** $M \subseteq E$ minimizing total cost $c(M) = \sum_{e \in M} c_e$.

### 2.2 Standard Assumptions (Without Loss of Generality)
1. **Balanced Sides ($|V| = |W| = n$):** If $|V| \ne |W|$, add dummy vertices to the smaller side.
2. **Existence of a Perfect Matching:** Add dummy complete bipartite edges with huge cost $C_{\max} \gg \sum_{e} |c_e|$.
3. **Non-Negative Edge Costs ($c_e \ge 0$):** Shifting every edge cost by $+K$ adds a constant $+nK$ to *every* perfect matching's cost, preserving the optimal perfect matching.

---

## 3. Structural Optimality Condition: Alternating Cycles

### 3.1 $M$-Alternating Paths and Cycles
Given a matching $M$:
- An **$M$-alternating path (or cycle)** is a simple path (or even-length cycle) whose edges alternate between belonging to $M$ and belonging to $E \setminus M$.
- If $C$ is an $M$-alternating cycle, toggling membership along $C$ produces a new matching $M' = M \triangle C = (M \setminus C) \cup (C \setminus M)$ where every vertex in $C$ still has degree $1$ in $M'$.

```text
      v_1 ======= w_1          Legend:
       |           |             ======= : Edge in M      (matched)
       |           |             ------- : Edge not in M  (unmatched)
      w_2 ======= v_2          Toggling C swaps ===== and -----,
                               changing cost by c(C \ M) - c(C ∩ M).
```

### 3.2 Negative Alternating Cycles & Optimality Theorem
> **Definition (Negative $M$-Alternating Cycle):** An $M$-alternating cycle $C$ is **negative** if the unmatched edges of $C$ are strictly cheaper than the matched edges of $C$:
> $$\sum_{e \in C \setminus M} c_e < \sum_{e \in C \cap M} c_e \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad c(M \triangle C) < c(M)$$

> **Theorem (Negative-Cycle Optimality Condition):** A perfect matching $M$ is a **minimum-cost perfect matching** if and only if $G$ contains **no negative $M$-alternating cycle**.

**Proof Sketch:**
- **$(\implies)$:** If a negative $M$-alternating cycle $C$ exists, then $M' = M \triangle C$ is another perfect matching with $c(M') < c(M)$.
- **$(\Longleftarrow)$:** Let $M^*$ be any other perfect matching. Look at the symmetric difference $M \oplus M^* = (M \setminus M^*) \cup (M^* \setminus M)$. In the subgraph $(V \cup W, M \oplus M^*)$, every vertex has degree $0$ (if matched to the same partner in both) or degree $2$ (one edge from $M$, one edge from $M^*$). Thus $M \oplus M^*$ decomposes into a disjoint union of $M$-alternating cycles $C_1, \dots, C_k$. Since no $C_i$ is negative, $\sum_{e \in C_i \setminus M} c_e \ge \sum_{e \in C_i \cap M} c_e$ for each $C_i$; summing over all $i$ yields $c(M^*) \ge c(M)$. $\blacksquare$

---

## 4. Vertex Prices, Reduced Costs, and Invariants

### 4.1 Reduced Costs
Assign a real-valued **price** (or dual weight) $p_u \in \mathbb{R}$ to every vertex $u \in V \cup W$. For each edge $e = (v, w) \in E$, define its **reduced cost** $c_e^p$ as:
$$c_e^p \triangleq c_e - p_v - p_w$$

### 4.2 The Two Hungarian Invariants
We maintain a (possibly partial) matching $M$ and vertex prices $p$ satisfying:
1. **Invariant 1 (Dual Feasibility / Non-Negative Reduced Costs):**
   $$c_e^p \ge 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad p_v + p_w \le c_{vw} \qquad \forall e = (v, w) \in E$$
2. **Invariant 2 (Complementary Slackness / Tight Matched Edges):**
   $$c_e^p = 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad p_v + p_w = c_{vw} \qquad \forall e = (v, w) \in M$$
   *(An edge $e$ with $c_e^p = 0$ is called **tight**.)*

### 4.3 Proof That the Invariants Guarantee Optimality at Termination
> **Claim:** If $M$ is a **perfect matching** and $(M, p)$ satisfies Invariants 1 and 2, then $M$ is a **minimum-cost perfect matching**.

**Proof:** Let $C$ be any $M$-alternating cycle. Because every vertex $u \in C$ is incident to **exactly one** edge in $C \cap M$ and **exactly one** edge in $C \setminus M$:
$$\sum_{e \in C \cap M} c_e = \sum_{e \in C \cap M} \underbrace{c_e^p}_{= 0 \text{ (Inv. 2)}} + \sum_{u \in C} p_u = \sum_{u \in C} p_u$$
$$\sum_{e \in C \setminus M} c_e = \sum_{e \in C \setminus M} \underbrace{c_e^p}_{\ge 0 \text{ (Inv. 1)}} + \sum_{u \in C} p_u \ge \sum_{u \in C} p_u = \sum_{e \in C \cap M} c_e$$
Thus $C$ cannot be a negative alternating cycle! By the Negative-Cycle Optimality Theorem, $M$ is optimal. $\blacksquare$

---

## 5. The Hungarian Algorithm: Good Paths vs. Good Sets

### 5.1 Initialization
Start with $M = \emptyset$ and $p_u = 0$ for all $u \in V \cup W$. Since $c_e \ge 0$, Invariants 1 and 2 hold trivially.

### 5.2 The Two Progress Cases (While $|M| < n$)

#### Case 1: There Exists a "Good Path" (Tight Augmenting Path)
A **good path** $P$ is an $M$-alternating path consisting **entirely of tight edges** ($c_e^p = 0$) from an unmatched left vertex $v_0 \in V$ to an unmatched right vertex $w_k \in W$.
- **Action:** Augment $M \leftarrow M \triangle P$ (toggling matched and unmatched edges along $P$) and keep prices $p$ unchanged.
- **Why Invariants Hold:** Invariant 1 is unchanged; Invariant 2 holds because every newly matched edge came from $P$, which only used tight edges ($c_e^p = 0$)! Cardinality $|M|$ increases by $1$.

#### Case 2: There Exists a "Good Set" (Tight Constricted Set $S \subseteq V$)
Let $N_{\text{tight}}(S) = \{w \in W : \exists v \in S \text{ with tight edge } (v, w) \text{ having } c_{vw}^p = 0\}$. A subset $S \subseteq V$ is a **good set** if:
1. $S$ contains at least one unmatched vertex, and every matched vertex in $S$ has its partner in $N_{\text{tight}}(S)$.
2. Every vertex in $N_{\text{tight}}(S)$ is matched to a partner in $S$ (hence $|N_{\text{tight}}(S)| < |S|$).

```text
         Left Side V                    Right Side W
    +-------------------+          +-------------------+
    |  Set S:           |          |  N_tight(S):      |
    |   (v_unmatched)   |   c^p=0  |                   |
    |   (v_1) ==========|==========|==> (w_1)          |  Price Update:
    |   (v_2) ----\     |          +-------------------+    p_v <- p_v + Δ  for v in S
    +--------------\----+          |  W \ N_tight(S):  |    p_w <- p_w - Δ  for w in N_tight(S)
                    \--- c^p > 0 ->|--> (w_2)          |
                                   +-------------------+
```

- **Action (Dual Price Update):** Choose step size $\Delta > 0$ as the smallest reduced cost between $S$ and $W \setminus N_{\text{tight}}(S)$:
  $$\Delta = \min_{v \in S,\, w \in W \setminus N_{\text{tight}}(S)} c_{vw}^p > 0$$
  *(Note: By Hall's Theorem and the existence of a perfect matching in $G$, at least one such edge $(v, w)$ must exist!)*
  Update prices:
  - $p_v \leftarrow p_v + \Delta$ for all $v \in S$
  - $p_w \leftarrow p_w - \Delta$ for all $w \in N_{\text{tight}}(S)$
- **Effect on Reduced Costs $c_{vw}^p = c_{vw} - p_v - p_w$:**
  - $v \in S, w \in N_{\text{tight}}(S)$: $(+\Delta) + (-\Delta) = 0 \implies c_{vw}^p$ unchanged (all matched edges remain tight!).
  - $v \notin S, w \in N_{\text{tight}}(S)$: $0 + (-\Delta) \implies c_{vw}^p$ **increases** by $\Delta$ (remains $\ge 0$, and no matched edge crosses here).
  - $v \in S, w \notin N_{\text{tight}}(S)$: $c_{vw}^p$ **decreases** by $\Delta$, and by choice of $\Delta$, stays $\ge 0$ while **creating at least one new tight edge**!
