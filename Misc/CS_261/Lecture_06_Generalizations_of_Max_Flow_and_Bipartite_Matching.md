## 2. Completing the Hungarian Algorithm ($O(m n^2)$ Time)

### 2.1 Finding a "Good Path" or a "Good Set" via Alternating BFS
Recall from Lecture 5 that at each step we maintain a matching $M$ and vertex prices $p$ such that all edges have $c_e^p = c_{vw} - p_v - p_w \ge 0$ (Invariant 1) and all matched edges $e \in M$ are **tight** ($c_e^p = 0$, Invariant 2).

How do we systematically find *either* a **Good Path** (tight $M$-alternating path between unmatched $v_0 \in V$ and unmatched $w_k \in W$) *or* a **Good Set** (constricted set $S \subseteq V$ in the tight subgraph)?

**Alternating BFS on the Tight Subgraph $G_{\text{tight}} = (V \cup W, \{e \in E : c_e^p = 0\})$:**
1. Pick an unmatched left vertex $v_0 \in V$ as the root (Level 0).
2. **Odd Steps (Left $V \to$ Right $W$):** From current left vertices, follow all **unmatched tight edges** to unvisited right vertices $w \in W$.
   - **If any reached $w \in W$ is unmatched:** Stop! The tree path from $v_0$ to $w$ is a **Good Path** $P$. Augment $M \leftarrow M \triangle P$.
3. **Even Steps (Right $W \to$ Left $V$):** If every newly reached $w \in W$ is already matched in $M$, follow its unique **matched edge** $(w, v') \in M$ (which is guaranteed to be tight by Invariant 2!) to reach $v' \in V$.
4. **When Alternating BFS Gets Stuck:** Let $S \subseteq V$ be all left vertices in the alternating BFS tree, and let $N_{\text{tight}}(S) \subseteq W$ be all right vertices in the tree:

```text
Level 0 (V):           [ v_0 ]  (unmatched root)
                        /   \    (tight unmatched edges)
Level 1 (W):        (w_1)   (w_2)
                      ||     ||  (tight matched edges in M)
Level 2 (V):        [v_1]   [v_2]
                     (no more tight edges leaving S = {v_0, v_1, v_2}!)
```

   - Then $N_{\text{tight}}(S) = \{w_1, w_2\}$ is paired 1-to-1 with $S \setminus \{v_0\}$, so $|N_{\text{tight}}(S)| = |S| - 1 < |S|$ and no tight edges leave $S$ to $W \setminus N_{\text{tight}}(S)$. Thus **$S$ is a Good Set**!

### 2.2 Progress & Runtime Analysis ($O(m n^2)$)
- When $S$ is a Good Set, we update $p_v \leftarrow p_v + \Delta$ for $v \in S$ and $p_w \leftarrow p_w - \Delta$ for $w \in N_{\text{tight}}(S)$, where $\Delta = \min_{v \in S, w \notin N_{\text{tight}}(S)} c_{vw}^p > 0$.
- **Crucial Observation:** All existing tree edges have one endpoint in $S$ and one in $N_{\text{tight}}(S)$, so their reduced costs remain $0$ (the entire current Alternating BFS tree remains intact!). Moreover, the bottleneck edge $(v^*, w^*)$ with $v^* \in S, w^* \notin N_{\text{tight}}(S)$ **becomes newly tight** ($c_{v^* w^*}^p = 0$).
- In the next step, $w^*$ enters the Alternating BFS tree:
  - If $w^*$ is unmatched $\implies$ immediate **Good Path** augmentation!
  - If $w^*$ is matched to $v'' \in V$ $\implies$ the tree grows by at least one new right vertex $w^*$ and one new left vertex $v''$.
- Since the tree can grow at most $n$ times before either exhausting $W$ or hitting an unmatched right vertex:
  - At most $n$ price updates occur per matching augmentation.
  - At most $n$ matching augmentations occur until $|M| = n$.
  - Each step takes $O(m)$ time $\implies$ **Total Time $= O(m n^2)$** (reducible to $O(m n \log n)$ using heaps/Dijkstra with Johnson-style vertex potentials).

## 4. Generalization 1: Minimum-Cost Flow

### 4.1 Problem Statement
- **Input:** Directed graph $G = (V, E)$, source $s$, sink $t$, target flow value $d \ge 0$, capacities $u_e \ge 0$, and per-unit edge costs $c_e \in \mathbb{R}$.
- **Goal:** Find a feasible $(s,t)$-flow $f$ of value $\text{val}(f) = d$ minimizing total cost:
  $$\min_{f} \sum_{e \in E} c_e f_e \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \text{val}(f) = d, \; 0 \le f_e \le u_e, \; \text{flow conservation}$$

### 4.2 Special Cases Captured by Min-Cost Flow
1. **$s$-$t$ Shortest Path:** Set $d = 1$ and $u_e = 1$ for all $e \in E$.
2. **Maximum Flow:** Set $c_e = 0$ for all $e \in E$ and binary search on $d$ (or add a backward edge $(t, s)$ with cost $-1, u_{ts} = \infty$ and find a min-cost circulation).
3. **Min-Cost Perfect Bipartite Matching:** Use the standard bipartite flow network ($u = 1$ on all edges, $d = n$), set $c_e = 0$ on edges incident to $s$ or $t$, and keep original costs $c_e$ on middle edges $(v, w)$.

### 4.3 Optimality Condition (Negative-Cost Cycles in $G_f$)
In the residual network $G_f$, a forward residual edge $(v, w)$ has cost $+c_{vw}$ and a backward ("undo") residual edge $(w, v)$ has cost $-c_{vw}$.
> **Theorem:** A feasible flow $f$ of value $d$ is a **minimum-cost flow** if and only if the residual network $G_f$ contains **no directed cycle of negative total cost**.

## 5. Generalizations 2 & 3: Non-Bipartite Matching & Blossom Shrinking

### 5.1 Why Non-Bipartite Matching Is Harder (Odd Cycles)
- In a triangle $K_3$ with unit edge capacities, fractional routing can assign $f_e = \frac{1}{2}$ to each of the 3 edges (total value $1.5$), whereas the maximum integral matching has size $1$. Thus naive Max-Flow/LP relaxations have an integrality gap on odd cycles!
- **Tutte-Berge Formula (Structural Certificate):** In any graph $G = (V, E)$,
  $$\max_{M} |M| = \min_{U \subseteq V} \frac{1}{2} \Big( |V| + |U| - \text{odd}(G \setminus U) \Big)$$
  where $\text{odd}(G \setminus U)$ is the number of connected components of $G \setminus U$ having an **odd** number of vertices.

### 5.2 Edmonds' Blossom Algorithm (1965)
When growing an alternating BFS tree in a non-bipartite graph, we can encounter an edge between two vertices at the **same even level**, creating an odd-length alternating cycle called a **blossom**:

```text
                        (w_1) === [v_1]
                       /               \
   [v_0] === (w_0) ---+                 +--- (Odd Cycle / "Blossom" B)
                       \               /     Contract B into a single
                        (w_2) === [v_2]      supernode [B] and recurse!
```

- **Key Theorem (Edmonds):** Contracting a blossom $B$ into a single supernode $v_B$ preserves the existence of an augmenting path: $G$ has an $M$-augmenting path iff $G / B$ has an $(M / B)$-augmenting path!
- **Landmark Significance:** In his 1965 paper *"Paths, Trees, and Flowers"*, Jack Edmonds popularized **polynomial-time solvability** as the formal mathematical definition of an "efficient/good algorithm."

### 5.3 Fast Greedy Heuristic for Massive Graphs ($\frac{1}{2}$-Approximation)
For graphs with millions/billions of edges where $O(mn)$ exact algorithms are too slow, sorting edges in decreasing order of weight $w_{e_1} \ge w_{e_2} \ge \dots \ge w_{e_m} > 0$ and greedily adding each edge if neither endpoint is matched runs in $O(m \log m)$ time and guarantees at least **$50\%$ of the maximum weight matching** ($\text{Greedy} \ge \frac{1}{2} \text{OPT}$):
- *Worst-case tight example:* A 3-edge path with weights $1, \, 1+\epsilon, \, 1$, where Greedy picks the middle edge ($1+\epsilon$) and blocks both outer edges (total $2$).
