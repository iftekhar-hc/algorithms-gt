## 2. Proof of Complementary Slackness

### 2.1 Recall: The Weak Duality Inequality Chain
For any primal feasible $x$ ($Ax \le b, \; x \ge 0$) and dual feasible $y$ ($A^\top y \ge c, \; y \ge 0$), Weak Duality follows from two inequalities:
$$c^\top x = \sum_{j=1}^n c_j x_j \;\underbrace{\le}_{(1)}\; \sum_{j=1}^n (A^\top y)_j x_j = y^\top A x = \sum_{i=1}^m y_i (Ax)_i \;\underbrace{\le}_{(2)}\; \sum_{i=1}^m y_i b_i = b^\top y$$

### 2.2 Why Complementary Slackness Forces Equality
Recall the two Complementary Slackness conditions:
- **(CS1):** For every $j \in \{1, \dots, n\}$, either $x_j = 0$ or $(A^\top y)_j = c_j$, so $c_j x_j = (A^\top y)_j x_j$ for every $j$. Summing over $j = 1, \dots, n$ makes inequality **(1) hold with equality**!
- **(CS2):** For every $i \in \{1, \dots, m\}$, either $y_i = 0$ or $(Ax)_i = b_i$, so $y_i (Ax)_i = y_i b_i$ for every $i$. Summing over $i = 1, \dots, m$ makes inequality **(2) hold with equality**!

Furthermore, once **Strong Duality** ($\text{OPT}(P) = \text{OPT}(D)$) is established, Complementary Slackness is **both necessary and sufficient** for a primal-dual feasible pair $(x, y)$ to be optimal!

## 3. Case Study: Min-Cost Bipartite Matching & the Hungarian Algorithm Revisited

### 3.1 Primal LP (Linear Relaxation of Min-Cost Perfect Matching)
Given bipartite graph $G = (V \cup W, E)$ with $|V| = |W| = n$ and edge costs $c_e$, relax $x_e \in \{0, 1\}$ to $x_e \ge 0$:
$$\begin{aligned}
\min \quad & \sum_{e \in E} c_e x_e \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & \sum_{e \in \delta(u)} x_e = 1 \qquad \forall u \in V \cup W \quad (\text{unrestricted dual variable } p_u \in \mathbb{R}) \\
& x_e \ge 0 \qquad \forall e \in E
\end{aligned}$$
*(Notice we do not even need to write $x_e \le 1$, because $x_e \ge 0$ and $\sum_{e \in \delta(u)} x_e = 1$ automatically force $x_e \le 1$!)*

### 3.2 Taking the Dual LP
Here the constraint matrix $A \in \{0, 1\}^{(|V|+|W|) \times |E|}$ is the vertex-edge incidence matrix: column $e = (v, w)$ has exactly two $1$s, in row $v$ and row $w$. Applying the mechanical duality recipe:
- Minimization $\implies$ Maximization.
- Equality constraints $= 1$ for each vertex $u \in V \cup W \implies$ **unrestricted real dual variables $p_u \in \mathbb{R}$** ("vertex prices").
- Non-negative primal variables $x_e \ge 0$ for each edge $e = (v, w) \implies$ dual inequality constraint $\le c_e$:

$$\begin{aligned}
\max_{p \in \mathbb{R}^{V \cup W}} \quad & \sum_{u \in V \cup W} p_u \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & p_v + p_w \le c_{vw} \qquad \forall e = (v, w) \in E
\end{aligned}$$

### 3.3 Decoding the Hungarian Algorithm via LP Duality

| LP Duality Concept | Translation in Min-Cost Matching | Role in Hungarian Algorithm |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dual Variables $p_u \in \mathbb{R}$** | Vertex prices $p_v, p_w$ | Maintained on all vertices $V \cup W$ |
| **Dual Slack $c_e - (A^\top p)_e$** | Reduced cost $c_e^p = c_{vw} - p_v - p_w$ | Guides which edges are usable |
| **Dual Feasibility ($A^\top p \le c$)** | $c_e^p \ge 0 \quad \forall e \in E$ | **Invariant 1** (always maintained) |
| **Complementary Slackness ($x_e > 0 \implies (A^\top p)_e = c_e$)** | $e \in M \implies c_e^p = 0$ (tight!) | **Invariant 2** (always maintained) |
| **Primal Feasibility ($Ax = \mathbf{1}, x \ge 0$)** | $M$ is a perfect matching ($|M| = n$) | **Termination condition** (achieved at end) |

> **The Primal-Dual Algorithm Design Paradigm:** To satisfy the three optimality conditions (**Primal Feasibility**, **Dual Feasibility**, **Complementary Slackness**), initialize with Dual Feasibility + Complementary Slackness and iteratively work toward Primal Feasibility!
>
> **Bonus Structural Consequence (Integrality of the Matching Polytope / Birkhoff-von Neumann):** Because the Hungarian Algorithm always terminates with an **integral ($0/1$) perfect matching $M^*$** and a dual feasible price vector $p^*$ of equal objective value, we have:
> $$\text{OPT}(\text{Dual LP}) \le \text{OPT}(\text{Primal Fractional LP}) \le \text{Cost}(M^*) = \text{OPT}(\text{Dual LP})$$
> Thus **every bipartite matching LP has an integral ($0/1$) optimal solution** (fractional matchings cannot beat the best integral matching!).

## 4. Strong Linear Programming Duality & Farkas's Lemma

### 4.1 Statement of Strong Duality
> **Theorem (Strong LP Duality — von Neumann, Gale-Kuhn-Tucker):** If a Primal LP $(P)$ and its Dual LP $(D)$ are **both feasible**, then both have finite optimal objective values and
> $$\text{OPT}(P) = \text{OPT}(D)$$

Combined with Weak Duality, for any Primal-Dual pair $(P, D)$, only **four** combinations of outcomes can ever occur:

| Primal $(P)$ \ Dual $(D)$ | **Optimal (Finite)** | **Infeasible** | **Unbounded** |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Optimal (Finite)** | **Possible** ($\text{OPT}(P) = \text{OPT}(D)$) | Impossible (by Strong Duality) | Impossible (by Weak Duality) |
| **Infeasible** | Impossible (by Strong Duality) | **Possible** | **Possible** |
| **Unbounded** | Impossible (by Weak Duality) | **Possible** | Impossible (by Weak Duality) |

### 4.2 Farkas's Lemma (1902): The Feasibility Version of Strong Duality
Just as Strong Duality says every optimal LP value has a linear-combination certificate, **Farkas's Lemma** says every **infeasible** linear system has a linear-combination certificate of infeasibility:

> **Theorem (Farkas's Lemma):** For any matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ and vector $b \in \mathbb{R}^m$, **exactly one** of the following two systems has a solution:
> - **System (1):** $\exists x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ such that $Ax = b$ and $x \ge 0$.
> - **System (2):** $\exists y \in \mathbb{R}^m$ such that $y^\top A \ge 0^\top$ and $y^\top b < 0$.

*(Why can't both hold? If both $x$ and $y$ existed, then $0 \le (y^\top A) x = y^\top (Ax) = y^\top b < 0$, a contradiction!)*

### 4.3 Geometric Proof of Farkas's Lemma via Separating Hyperplanes
Suppose System (1) is **infeasible**. We must construct $y \in \mathbb{R}^m$ satisfying System (2).

Define the **convex cone of all feasible right-hand sides**:
$$Q = \{Ax : x \in \mathbb{R}^n, \; x \ge 0\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$$
Equivalently, $Q$ is the set of all non-negative linear combinations of the $n$ columns $A^{(1)}, \dots, A^{(n)}$ of $A$.

```text
                        /  Cone Q = { A x : x >= 0 }
                       /   (all non-negative combinations of columns of A)
                      /
                     /     * A^(1)
          (0)-------+          * A^(2)
              \      \
               \      \________________________
       Hyperplane      
     alpha^T z = 0        * b  (infeasible right-hand side: alpha^T b < 0)
```

1. **Apply the Separating Hyperplane Theorem:** Since $Q \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$ is closed and convex and $b \notin Q$ (because System (1) is infeasible), there exists a normal vector $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}^m$ and threshold $\beta \in \mathbb{R}$ (which can be taken as $\beta = 0$ since $Q$ is a cone containing $0$ and closed under positive scaling) such that:
   $$\alpha^\top d \ge 0 \quad \forall d \in Q, \qquad \text{and} \qquad \alpha^\top b < 0$$
2. **Verify $y = \alpha$ Satisfies System (2):**
   - Clearly $y^\top b = \alpha^\top b < 0$.
   - For each column $j \in \{1, \dots, n\}$, the $j$-th column $A^{(j)} = A e_j$ belongs to $Q$ (by setting $x = e_j \ge 0$). Therefore $\alpha^\top A^{(j)} \ge 0$ for every $j = 1, \dots, n$, which in vector form is $\alpha^\top A \ge 0^\top$! $\blacksquare$
