## 1. Executive Summary

This lecture introduces **Linear Programming Duality**—the unifying mathematical framework behind every optimality condition in CS261 (including the Max-Flow Min-Cut Theorem and the Hungarian Algorithm's vertex prices):
1. **Motivation via Upper Bounds (Certificates of Optimality):** How to systematically prove an upper bound on a maximization LP by taking non-negative linear combinations of its constraints.
2. **The Primal-Dual LP Pair:** How minimizing that upper bound over all valid constraint multipliers $y \ge 0$ defines a new Linear Program—the **Dual LP**—where roles of variables and constraints are transposed ($A \leftrightarrow A^\top, \; b \leftrightarrow c$).
3. **Max-Flow Path LP & Fractional Min-Cut Dual:** Taking the dual of the path-based Max-Flow LP yields the fractional $(s,t)$-cut LP, directly recovering Weak Duality for flows and cuts.
4. **General Duality Recipe, Weak Duality Theorem, and Complementary Slackness:** The mechanical conversion table for general LPs (with $=, \le, \ge$ and free variables) and the **Complementary Slackness** optimality conditions.

## 2. Motivating Duality: Certifying Upper Bounds on an LP

### 2.1 A Concrete 2D Example
Consider the Primal LP $(P)$:
$$\begin{aligned}
\max \quad & x_1 + x_2 \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & 4x_1 + x_2 \le 2 \qquad (\text{Constraint } 1, \text{ multiplier } y_1 \ge 0) \\
& x_1 + 2x_2 \le 1 \qquad (\text{Constraint } 2, \text{ multiplier } y_2 \ge 0) \\
& x_1, x_2 \ge 0
\end{aligned}$$

How can we prove an upper bound on $x_1 + x_2$ for *every* feasible $(x_1, x_2)$?
- **Attempt 1 ($y_1 = 1, y_2 = 0$):** Since $x_1 \ge 0$, $x_1 + x_2 \le 4x_1 + x_2 \le 2$.
- **Attempt 2 ($y_1 = 0, y_2 = 1$):** Since $x_2 \ge 0$, $x_1 + x_2 \le x_1 + 2x_2 \le 1$.
- **Attempt 3 ($y_1 = \frac{1}{7}, y_2 = \frac{3}{7}$):** Take $\frac{1}{7} \times (\text{Constraint } 1) + \frac{3}{7} \times (\text{Constraint } 2)$:
  $$x_1 + x_2 = \frac{1}{7}(4x_1 + x_2) + \frac{3}{7}(x_1 + 2x_2) \le \frac{1}{7}(2) + \frac{3}{7}(1) = \frac{5}{7}$$
  Since $(x_1^*, x_2^*) = (\frac{3}{7}, \frac{2}{7})$ is feasible and achieves $x_1^* + x_2^* = \frac{5}{7}$, the multipliers $y^* = (\frac{1}{7}, \frac{3}{7})$ provide an **airtight, easily verifiable certificate** that $\text{OPT} = \frac{5}{7}$!

### 2.2 Generalizing to Arbitrary Canonical LPs
Given a **Primal LP $(P)$** in canonical maximization form:
$$\text{(Primal } P\text{)} \qquad \max \; c^\top x \quad \text{s.t.} \quad Ax \le b, \; x \ge 0$$
choose non-negative constraint multipliers $y = (y_1, \dots, y_m)^\top \ge 0$ such that the combined left-hand side dominates the objective coefficient vector $c$ coordinatewise ($A^\top y \ge c$). Then for **any** primal feasible $x \ge 0$:
$$c^\top x \le (A^\top y)^\top x = y^\top (Ax) \le y^\top b = b^\top y$$

To get the **tightest (smallest) possible upper bound**, we minimize $b^\top y$ over all valid multipliers $y$, yielding the **Dual LP $(D)$**:
$$\text{(Dual } D\text{)} \qquad \min \; b^\top y \quad \text{s.t.} \quad A^\top y \ge c, \; y \ge 0$$

```text
       PRIMAL LP (P)                              DUAL LP (D)
  +----------------------+                   +----------------------+
  |  Maximize   c^T x    |  <--- roles --->  |  Minimize   b^T y    |
  |  subject to A x <= b |    transposed     |  subject to A^T y >= c|
  |             x >= 0   |                   |             y >= 0   |
  +----------------------+                   +----------------------+
   n variables, m constraints                 m variables, n constraints
```

## 3. Case Study: Dual of the Path-Formulation Max-Flow LP

### 3.1 Primal LP (Path Decomposition Form)
Let $\mathcal{P}$ be the set of all simple $s \leadsto t$ paths in $G = (V, E)$, and introduce one variable $f_P \ge 0$ per path $P \in \mathcal{P}$:
$$\begin{aligned}
\max \quad & \sum_{P \in \mathcal{P}} 1 \cdot f_P \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & \sum_{P \in \mathcal{P} : e \in P} f_P \le u_e \qquad \forall e \in E \quad (\text{dual multiplier } \ell_e \ge 0) \\
& f_P \ge 0 \qquad \forall P \in \mathcal{P}
\end{aligned}$$
Here the constraint matrix $A \in \{0, 1\}^{|E| \times |\mathcal{P}|}$ has rows indexed by edges $e \in E$, columns indexed by paths $P \in \mathcal{P}$, and entry $A_{e, P} = 1 \iff e \in P$.

### 3.2 Dual LP (Edge Lengths / Fractional $(s,t)$-Cut)
Transposing $A$ gives one dual variable $\ell_e \ge 0$ ("edge length") per edge $e \in E$ and one constraint per path $P \in \mathcal{P}$:
$$\begin{aligned}
\min \quad & \sum_{e \in E} u_e \ell_e \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & \sum_{e \in P} \ell_e \ge 1 \qquad \forall P \in \mathcal{P} \\
& \ell_e \ge 0 \qquad \forall e \in E
\end{aligned}$$
- **Geometric / Physical Interpretation:** Assign non-negative lengths $\ell_e \ge 0$ to edges so that $s$ and $t$ have shortest-path distance $\ge 1$, while minimizing the total "pipe volume" $\sum_{e} u_e \ell_e$.
- **Connection to $(s,t)$-Cuts:** For any $(s,t)$-cut $(A, B)$, setting $\ell_e = 1$ for $e \in \delta^+(A)$ and $\ell_e = 0$ otherwise is **dual feasible** (since every $s \leadsto t$ path $P$ must cross $\delta^+(A)$ at least once, so $\sum_{e \in P} \ell_e \ge 1$) and has dual objective value $\sum_{e \in \delta^+(A)} u_e = c(A, B)$!
- By the Max-Flow Min-Cut Theorem, even though the Dual LP allows fractional lengths $\ell_e \in [0, 1]$, its minimum is achieved by an integral $0/1$ cut vector!

## 4. General Duality Recipe, Weak Duality, and Complementary Slackness

### 4.1 Complete Mechanical Duality Conversion Table

| Primal (Maximization) | $\Longleftrightarrow$ | Dual (Minimization) |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| $i$-th constraint is $\le b_i$ | $\Longleftrightarrow$ | $y_i \ge 0$ |
| $i$-th constraint is $\ge b_i$ | $\Longleftrightarrow$ | $y_i \le 0$ |
| $i$-th constraint is $= b_i$ | $\Longleftrightarrow$ | $y_i \in \mathbb{R}$ (unrestricted / free) |
| $x_j \ge 0$ | $\Longleftrightarrow$ | $j$-th dual constraint is $\ge c_j$ |
| $x_j \le 0$ | $\Longleftrightarrow$ | $j$-th dual constraint is $\le c_j$ |
| $x_j \in \mathbb{R}$ (unrestricted / free) | $\Longleftrightarrow$ | $j$-th dual constraint is $= c_j$ (tight!) |

*(Why does an equality constraint correspond to an unrestricted dual variable? Because you can scale an equation $a_i^\top x = b_i$ by either a positive or negative multiplier $y_i \in \mathbb{R}$ without flipping an inequality!)*

### 4.2 Weak Duality Theorem & Corollaries
> **Theorem (Weak LP Duality):** For any primal feasible $x$ (in a maximization LP $P$) and any dual feasible $y$ (in its dual minimization LP $D$),
> $$c^\top x \le b^\top y$$

```text
  Primal Values (c^T x)                    Dual Values (b^T y)
  ------x-------x------x------[x* / y*]------o------o-------o------> ℝ
   (Maximization: <= OPT)     No Gap!      (Minimization: >= OPT)
```

**Three Immediate Corollaries of Weak Duality:**
1. If Primal $(P)$ is **unbounded** ($\text{OPT}(P) = +\infty$), then Dual $(D)$ must be **infeasible**.
2. If Dual $(D)$ is **unbounded** ($\text{OPT}(D) = -\infty$), then Primal $(P)$ must be **infeasible**.
   *(Note: It is possible for **both** $(P)$ and $(D)$ to be infeasible simultaneously!)*
3. **Certificate of Optimality:** If $x$ is primal feasible, $y$ is dual feasible, and $c^\top x = b^\top y$, then $x$ and $y$ are **both optimal** for $(P)$ and $(D)$ respectively.

### 4.3 Complementary Slackness Conditions
> **Corollary (Complementary Slackness):** Let $x$ and $y$ be feasible for $(P)$ and $(D)$. Suppose:
> - **(CS1 — Primal Variable $\implies$ Tight Dual Constraint):** For every $j \in \{1, \dots, n\}$,
>   $$x_j \ne 0 \implies (A^\top y)_j = c_j \quad \text{(i.e., } x_j (c_j - (A^\top y)_j) = 0\text{)}$$
> - **(CS2 — Dual Variable $\implies$ Tight Primal Constraint):** For every $i \in \{1, \dots, m\}$,
>   $$y_i \ne 0 \implies (Ax)_i = b_i \quad \text{(i.e., } y_i (b_i - (Ax)_i) = 0\text{)}$$
>
> Then $c^\top x = b^\top y$, so **$x$ and $y$ are both optimal**! *(Proved in Lecture 9; directly explains both Max-Flow Min-Cut saturation conditions and the Hungarian Algorithm's tight-edge invariants!)*
