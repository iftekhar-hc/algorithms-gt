## 1. Executive Summary

This lecture bridges Part II (Linear Programming & Zero-Sum Games) and Part III (Online Algorithms) by introducing the **Online Decision-Making (Experts) Framework** and one of the most versatile algorithms in all of computer science: the **Multiplicative Weights Update (MWU) Algorithm** (also known as Randomized Weighted Majority / Hedge).

Key highlights:
1. **Online Decision Making & Regret:** Why competing with the best *switching* sequence of actions in hindsight is impossible, why *deterministic* algorithms suffer $\Omega(T)$ regret even against the best *fixed* action in hindsight, and why **sublinear regret $o(T)$** ("no-regret learning") is the gold-standard benchmark.
2. **The Multiplicative Weights Algorithm:** Maintain positive weights $w_a^{(t)}$ over $n$ actions, choose actions proportional to $w_a^{(t)}$, and update $w_a^{(t+1)} = w_a^{(t)} (1 + \eta r_a^{(t)})$.
3. **Complete Potential-Function Proof of $O(\sqrt{T \ln n})$ Regret:** Relating the total weight $\Gamma_t = \sum_{a \in A} w_a^{(t)}$ to both the algorithm's expected cumulative reward (upper bound via $1 + x \le e^x$) and the best fixed action's cumulative reward (lower bound via $\ln(1 + x) \ge x - x^2$).
4. **Algorithmic Proof of Von Neumann's Minimax Theorem:** Two players playing a zero-sum game using Multiplicative Weights converge in $O(\frac{\ln n}{\epsilon^2})$ rounds to an $\epsilon$-approximate minimax equilibrium.

## 2. The Online Decision-Making ("Prediction with Expert Advice") Model

### 2.1 Protocol over Time Horizon $t = 1, 2, \dots, T$
Let $A = \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$ be a fixed set of $n$ actions (or "experts" / "strategies"). On each day $t = 1, \dots, T$:
1. **Algorithm Commits:** Knowing only past reward vectors $r^{(1)}, \dots, r^{(t-1)}$, the algorithm chooses a probability distribution $p^{(t)} \in \Delta_n$ over actions (and samples an action $a^{(t)} \sim p^{(t)}$).
2. **Adversary Reveals Rewards:** An adversary reveals the day-$t$ reward vector $r^{(t)} \in [-1, +1]^n$, where $r_a^{(t)} \in [-1, +1]$ is the reward of action $a$ on day $t$.
3. **Algorithm Earns Expected Reward:** $\gamma_t = \sum_{a \in A} p_a^{(t)} r_a^{(t)}$.

```text
   Day t = 1, 2, ..., T:
   +-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
   | 1. Learner picks distribution p^(t) over n actions (using r^(1..t-1)) |
   | 2. Nature/Adversary reveals reward vector r^(t) in [-1, 1]^n          |
   | 3. Learner receives expected reward  γ_t = (p^(t))^T r^(t)            |
   +-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 2.2 What Benchmark Can We Compete Against? (Impossibility Results)
1. **Omniscient Switching Benchmark $\sum_{t=1}^T \max_{a \in A} r_a^{(t)}$ is Impossible:**  
   Even for $n = 2$ actions, an adversary who knows the algorithm's distribution $p^{(t)} = (p_1^{(t)}, p_2^{(t)})$ can set $r^{(t)} = (-1, +1)$ whenever $p_1^{(t)} \ge 1/2$ and $r^{(t)} = (+1, -1)$ whenever $p_1^{(t)} < 1/2$. The algorithm gets $\le 0$ every day, while the switching benchmark gets $+T$.
2. **Best Fixed Action in Hindsight ($\text{OPT} = \max_{a^* \in A} \sum_{t=1}^T r_{a^*}^{(t)}$):**  
    We define the **Regret** of an algorithm over $T$ rounds as:
   $$\text{Regret}_T \triangleq \max_{a^* \in A} \sum_{t=1}^T r_{a^*}^{(t)} - \sum_{t=1}^T \gamma_t$$
   - **Why Deterministic Algorithms Fail (e.g., "Follow the Leader"):** If an algorithm deterministically picks $a^{(t)}$ on day $t$, the adversary sets $r_{a^{(t)}}^{(t)} = 0$ and $r_{a'}^{(t)} = 1$ for $a' \ne a^{(t)}$. For $n = 2$, alternating which action gets $0$ vs. $1$ gives the algorithm total reward $0$, while the best fixed action gets $\ge T/2 \implies \text{Regret}_T \ge T/2 = \Omega(T)$!
   - **Goal (No-Regret Learning):** Design a **randomized** algorithm whose time-averaged regret $\frac{1}{T}\text{Regret}_T \to 0$ as $T \to \infty$ (specifically, $\text{Regret}_T = O(\sqrt{T \ln n})$).

## 3. The Multiplicative Weights Update (MWU) Algorithm & Regret Proof

### 3.1 Algorithm Pseudocode

```text
MULTIPLICATIVE-WEIGHTS(Actions A = {1..n}, Time Horizon T, Learning Rate η in (0, 1/2]):
    Initialize weights: w_a^(1) = 1  for all a in A

    for t = 1, 2, ..., T:
        1. Normalize weights into a probability distribution:
               Γ_t = sum_{a in A} w_a^(t)
               p_a^(t) = w_a^(t) / Γ_t    for all a in A
        2. Play action a^(t) sampled according to p^(t)
        3. Observe reward vector r^(t) in [-1, +1]^n
        4. Multiplicative Weight Update:
               w_a^(t+1) = w_a^(t) * (1 + η * r_a^(t))    for all a in A
```

### 3.2 Complete Regret Analysis via the Potential Function $\Gamma_t = \sum_{a \in A} w_a^{(t)}$
> **Theorem (MWU Regret Bound):** For any $\eta \in (0, \frac{1}{2}]$ and any adversarial sequence $r^{(1)}, \dots, r^{(T)} \in [-1, 1]^n$,
> $$\sum_{t=1}^T \gamma_t \ge \max_{a^* \in A} \sum_{t=1}^T r_{a^*}^{(t)} - \eta T - \frac{\ln n}{\eta}$$
> Setting the optimal learning rate $\eta = \sqrt{\frac{\ln n}{T}}$ yields **$\text{Regret}_T \le 2\sqrt{T \ln n}$** (or time-averaged regret $\le \epsilon$ after $T = O(\frac{\ln n}{\epsilon^2})$ rounds).

**Proof (Two-Sided Bound on the Potential Function $\Gamma_{T+1} = \sum_{a \in A} w_a^{(T+1)}$):**

#### Step 1: Upper-Bounding $\Gamma_{T+1}$ in Terms of MWU's Expected Reward $\sum_{t=1}^T \gamma_t$
On day $t$, MWU's expected reward is $\gamma_t = \sum_{a \in A} p_a^{(t)} r_a^{(t)} = \frac{1}{\Gamma_t} \sum_{a \in A} w_a^{(t)} r_a^{(t)}$. Therefore:
$$\Gamma_{t+1} = \sum_{a \in A} w_a^{(t+1)} = \sum_{a \in A} w_a^{(t)} \big(1 + \eta r_a^{(t)}\big) = \Gamma_t + \eta \underbrace{\sum_{a \in A} w_a^{(t)} r_a^{(t)}}_{= \Gamma_t \gamma_t} = \Gamma_t (1 + \eta \gamma_t) \le \Gamma_t e^{\eta \gamma_t}$$
where we used $1 + x \le e^x$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$. Unrolling from $t = 1$ to $T$ (with $\Gamma_1 = \sum_{a \in A} 1 = n$):
$$\Gamma_{T+1} \le \Gamma_1 \prod_{t=1}^T e^{\eta \gamma_t} = n \cdot \exp\left(\eta \sum_{t=1}^T \gamma_t\right)$$

#### Step 2: Lower-Bounding $\Gamma_{T+1}$ in Terms of the Best Fixed Action $a^* \in A$
Since all weights are positive, $\Gamma_{T+1} = \sum_{a \in A} w_a^{(T+1)} \ge w_{a^*}^{(T+1)} = \prod_{t=1}^T \big(1 + \eta r_{a^*}^{(t)}\big)$.

#### Step 3: Combining the Upper and Lower Bounds
Taking natural logarithms of $w_{a^*}^{(T+1)} \le \Gamma_{T+1} \le n \exp\big(\eta \sum_{t=1}^T \gamma_t\big)$ and using the Taylor estimate $\ln(1 + x) \ge x - x^2$ for all $x \in [-\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}]$ (since $|\eta r_{a^*}^{(t)}| \le \eta \le \frac{1}{2}$):
$$\ln n + \eta \sum_{t=1}^T \gamma_t \ge \ln \Gamma_{T+1} \ge \sum_{t=1}^T \ln\big(1 + \eta r_{a^*}^{(t)}\big) \ge \sum_{t=1}^T \left(\eta r_{a^*}^{(t)} - \eta^2 \big(r_{a^*}^{(t)}\big)^2\right) \ge \eta \sum_{t=1}^T r_{a^*}^{(t)} - \eta^2 T$$
Dividing both sides by $\eta > 0$ and rearranging gives:
$$\sum_{t=1}^T \gamma_t \ge \sum_{t=1}^T r_{a^*}^{(t)} - \eta T - \frac{\ln n}{\eta} \quad \blacksquare$$

## 4. Algorithmic Proof of the Minimax Theorem via MWU

Given a zero-sum game $A \in [-1, 1]^{m \times n}$, simulate $T = \lceil \frac{4 \ln(\max(m, n))}{\epsilon^2} \rceil$ rounds of repeated play:
- **Row Player** runs MWU over the $m$ rows; on day $t$, after Column plays $q^{(t)} \in \Delta_n$, Row receives reward vector $r_{\text{row}}^{(t)} = A q^{(t)}$.
- **Column Player** runs MWU (or best response) over the $n$ columns; on day $t$, after Row plays $p^{(t)} \in \Delta_m$, Column receives reward vector $r_{\text{col}}^{(t)} = -(p^{(t)})^\top A$.

Let $\hat{x} = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T p^{(t)} \in \Delta_m$, $\hat{y} = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T q^{(t)} \in \Delta_n$, and $V = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T (p^{(t)})^\top A q^{(t)}$. By the MWU regret guarantee for Row and Column:
$$\max_{x \in \Delta_m} \min_{y \in \Delta_n} x^\top A y \ge \min_{y \in \Delta_n} \hat{x}^\top A y \ge V - \epsilon \ge \max_{x \in \Delta_m} x^\top A \hat{y} - 2\epsilon \ge \min_{y \in \Delta_n} \max_{x \in \Delta_m} x^\top A y - 2\epsilon$$
Letting $\epsilon \to 0$ proves $\max_{x} \min_{y} x^\top A y = \min_{y} \max_{x} x^\top A y$!
