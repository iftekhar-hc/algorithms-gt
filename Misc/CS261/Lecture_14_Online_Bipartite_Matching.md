## 1. Executive Summary

This lecture culminates the Online Algorithms module with **Online Bipartite Matching**—the mathematical backbone of modern online search/display ad allocation (Karp, Vazirani, Vazirani 1990; Mehta, Saberi, Vazirani, Vazirani 2005; Devanur, Jain, Kleinberg 2013):
1. **Deterministic Barrier ($\frac{1}{2}$):** No deterministic online algorithm can achieve a competitive ratio better than $\frac{1}{2}$ (by a 4-node adversary argument), and **Deterministic Greedy** achieves $\frac{1}{2}$ (proven cleanly via **Weak LP Duality**).
2. **Hedging via Fractional Matching (The Water-Filling / BALANCE Algorithm):** When an online right vertex $w \in R$ arrives, it continuously pours 1 unit of "water" into its neighbors $v \in L$ having the **lowest current water level $y_v$** (until either $w$'s 1 unit is exhausted or all neighbors reach capacity $1$).
3. **Primal-Dual Analysis & Differential Equation Derivation of $1 - \frac{1}{e} \approx 63.2\%$:** By splitting each infinitesimal primal increase $dz$ on edge $(v, w)$ unequally between dual variables $q_v$ (receiving $g(y_v) dz$) and $q_w$ (receiving $(1 - g(y_v)) dz$) where $g(y) = e^{y-1}$ solves $g'(y) = g(y)$, we prove that $\frac{e}{e-1} q$ is **dual feasible**, establishing the optimal **$1 - \frac{1}{e}$ competitive ratio**!

## 2. Deterministic Greedy Achieves $\frac{1}{2}$ via Weak LP Duality

### 2.1 Why No Deterministic Algorithm Beats $\frac{1}{2}$
Let $L = \{v_1, v_2\}$. First online vertex $w_1 \in R$ arrives with edges to both $\{v_1, v_2\}$.
- If a deterministic algorithm matches $(v_1, w_1)$, the adversary sends $w_2$ connected **only** to $v_1$ (algorithm gets $1$, $\text{OPT} = 2$).
- If it matches $(v_2, w_1)$, the adversary sends $w_2$ connected **only** to $v_2$ (algorithm gets $1$, $\text{OPT} = 2$).

### 2.2 Primal-Dual Pair for Maximum Cardinality Bipartite Matching

```text
     PRIMAL LP (Fractional Matching)                DUAL LP (Fractional Vertex Cover)
+-----------------------------------------+     +-----------------------------------------+
| max   sum_{e in E} x_e                  |     | min   sum_{v in L} p_v + sum_{w in R} p_w|
| s.t.  sum_{e in δ(u)} x_e <= 1  (∀ u)   | <-> | s.t.  p_v + p_w >= 1   ∀ (v, w) in E    |
|       x_e >= 0                  (∀ e)   |     |       p_v, p_w >= 0    ∀ v in L, w in R |
+-----------------------------------------+     +-----------------------------------------+
```

### 2.3 Dual-Scaling Proof That Deterministic Greedy Is $\frac{1}{2}$-Competitive
Whenever Greedy adds an edge $e = (v, w)$ to its matching $M$ ( raising $x_{vw}$ from $0$ to $1$), define pre-dual vertex weights $q_v = \frac{1}{2}$ and $q_w = \frac{1}{2}$ (and $q_u = 0$ for all unmatched vertices).
1. **Primal-Dual Value Equality:** By construction, every matched edge splits its value $1 = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}$ between its two endpoints:
   $$|M| = \sum_{u \in L \cup R} q_u$$
2. **Approximate Dual Feasibility:** For **every** edge $(v, w) \in E$ in the graph, at the moment $w$ arrived, either $v$ was already matched ($q_v = \frac{1}{2}$) or $w$ became matched to $v$ or another neighbor ($q_w = \frac{1}{2}$). Thus **at least one endpoint of $(v, w)$ is matched**, implying:
   $$q_v + q_w \ge \frac{1}{2} \qquad \forall (v, w) \in E$$
3. **Scaling to True Dual Feasibility:** Setting $p_u = 2 q_u$ satisfies $p_v + p_w = 2(q_v + q_w) \ge 1$ for all $(v, w) \in E$, so $p$ is **dual feasible**! By Weak LP Duality:
   $$\text{OPT} \le \sum_{u \in L \cup R} p_u = 2 \sum_{u \in L \cup R} q_u = 2 |M| \quad \implies \quad |M| \ge \frac{1}{2} \text{OPT} \quad \blacksquare$$

## 3. Beating $\frac{1}{2}$: The Water-Filling (BALANCE) Algorithm

### 3.1 Intuition: Hedging Bets via Equal Water Levels
In the 4-node bad example, when $w_1$ arrives connected to both $v_1$ and $v_2$, instead of committing $100\%$ to $v_1$ or $v_2$, a fractional algorithm can hedge by matching $x_{v_1 w_1} = \frac{1}{2}$ and $x_{v_2 w_1} = \frac{1}{2}$ (Then when $w_2$ arrives connected only to $v_1$, $v_1$ still has $\frac{1}{2}$ capacity left, achieving total value $1.5/2 = 75\% > 50\%$!).

### 3.2 The Water-Filling Algorithm
Think of each left vertex $v \in L$ as a bucket of capacity $1$, with current **water level** $y_v = \sum_{w \in \Gamma(v)} x_{vw} \in [0, 1]$, and each arriving right vertex $w \in R$ as bringing $1$ liter of water to pour into its compatible buckets $\Gamma(w) \subseteq L$:

> **Water-Filling Rule:** When $w \in R$ arrives, continuously pour its $1$ unit of water into the bucket(s) $v \in \Gamma(w)$ that currently have the **lowest water level $y_v$** (raising tied lowest buckets at the same rate) until **either**:
> - **Case 1:** All compatible buckets $v \in \Gamma(w)$ become completely full ($y_v = 1$), or
> - **Case 2:** All $1$ unit of $w$'s water is distributed ($\sum_{v \in \Gamma(w)} x_{vw} = 1$).

```text
    Capacity 1.0  +-------+     +-------+     +-------+
                  |       |     |       |     |       |
    Common Level  |~~~~~~~|     |~~~~~~~|     |///////|  (already higher; gets 0 from w)
         y*       |new w  |     |new w  |     |///////|
                  |-------|     |-------|     |///////|
                  |old y_1|     |old y_2|     |old y_3|
                  +-------+     +-------+     +-------+
                   Bucket v1     Bucket v2     Bucket v3
```

**Key Structural Property of Water-Filling:**
For any edge $(v, w) \in E$, if $v$'s **final** water level at the end of the algorithm is $y_v < 1$, then at the moment $w$ arrived, $v$ was not full, so $w$ must have distributed **all 1 unit** of its water (Case 2), and every drop of water $w$ poured into any neighbor $v'$ entered at a water level $z \le y_v$!

## 4. Primal-Dual Proof of $\left(1 - \frac{1}{e}\right)$-Competitiveness

### 4.1 Why a 50-50 Split Fails, and How Level-Dependent Splitting Works
If we split every increase $dx_{vw} = dz$ equally ($50$-$50$) between $q_v$ and $q_w$, a full left vertex ($y_v = 1$) connected to an empty right vertex ($q_w = 0$) only has $q_v + q_w = \frac{1}{2} + 0 = \frac{1}{2}$.
- **Solution (Level-Dependent Unequal Split):** Choose a non-decreasing function $g : [0, 1] \to [0, 1]$. Whenever edge $(v, w)$ is increased by an infinitesimal amount $dz$ while $v$ is at water level $z \in [0, 1]$:
  - Give **$g(z) \, dz$** to the left endpoint $q_v$.
  - Give **$(1 - g(z)) \, dz$** to the right endpoint $q_w$.
- Clearly $\sum_{u \in L \cup R} q_u = \sum_{e \in E} x_e$ (since $g(z) + (1 - g(z)) = 1$). We want to choose $g$ to maximize the minimum possible value of $q_v + q_w$ over all edges $(v, w) \in E$!

### 4.2 Bounding $q_v + q_w$ and Deriving the Differential Equation $g'(y) = g(y)$
Consider any edge $(v, w) \in E$, and let $y = y_v \in [0, 1]$ be the **final** water level of $v$:
1. **Case 1 ($y_v = 1$ — Left Endpoint $v$ Ends Full):** Even if $q_w = 0$, $v$ accumulated shares from level $0$ to $1$:
   $$q_v + q_w \ge q_v = \int_0^1 g(z) \, dz \qquad \text{(Expression 1)}$$
2. **Case 2 ($y_v < 1$ — Left Endpoint $v$ Ends Partially Full at Level $y = y_v$):**
   - Left endpoint $v$ accumulated $q_v = \int_0^{y} g(z) \, dz$.
   - By the Key Structural Property of Water-Filling, $w$ poured **all $1$ unit** of its water into buckets at levels $z \le y$. Since $g$ is non-decreasing, $1 - g(z) \ge 1 - g(y)$ for every drop poured by $w$, so $q_w \ge 1 \cdot (1 - g(y))$.
   - Combining $q_v$ and $q_w$:
     $$q_v + q_w \ge \left(\int_0^y g(z) \, dz\right) + 1 - g(y) \qquad \text{(Expression 2)}$$

**Solving for the Optimal Splitting Function $g(y)$:**
- To make Expression 2 constant (independent of $y \in [0, 1]$), differentiate with respect to $y$ and set to $0$:
  $$\frac{d}{dy}\left(\int_0^y g(z) \, dz + 1 - g(y)\right) = g(y) - g'(y) = 0 \quad \implies \quad g'(y) = g(y) \quad \implies \quad g(y) = k \cdot e^y$$
- Plug $g(y) = k e^y$ into Expression 1 and Expression 2 and equate them:
  - $\text{Expression 1} = \int_0^1 k e^z \, dz = k(e - 1)$
  - $\text{Expression 2} = \int_0^y k e^z \, dz + 1 - k e^y = k(e^y - 1) + 1 - k e^y = 1 - k$
  - Equating $k(e - 1) = 1 - k \implies k e = 1 \implies \mathbf{k = \frac{1}{e}}$, so **$\mathbf{g(y) = e^{y-1}}$**!
- With $g(y) = e^{y-1}$, both Expression 1 and Expression 2 equal:
  $$q_v + q_w \ge 1 - \frac{1}{e} \approx 0.6321 \qquad \forall (v, w) \in E$$
- Scaling $p = \frac{e}{e-1} q$ yields a **feasible dual solution**, proving $\text{ALG} \ge \left(1 - \frac{1}{e}\right) \text{OPT}$! (For integral matching, the randomized **RANKING** algorithm picks a random permutation of $L$ up front and achieves the exact same $1 - \frac{1}{e}$ competitive ratio, which is worst-case optimal across all randomized online algorithms!).
