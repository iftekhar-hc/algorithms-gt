== Background: Convexity, Strong Convexity, and Gradient Descent Rates

A differentiable function stem:[f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}] is *convex* if

[stem]
++++
f(y)\ge f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle
++++

for all stem:[x,y]. This means the graph of stem:[f] lies above its tangents. Gradient descent (GD)

[stem]
++++
x_{t+1}=x_t-\eta_t\nabla f(x_t)
++++

on a convex Lipschitz function (with stem:[\|\nabla f\|\le G]) yields the classic rate

[stem]
++++
f\Bigl(\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T x_t\Bigr)-f(x^*)=O\Bigl(\frac{G\|x_1-x^*\|}{\sqrt T}\Bigr)
++++

(or stem:[O(G^2/\sqrt T)] after choosing a suitable constant step size). The stem:[1/\sqrt T] dependence is optimal in the worst case for merely convex functions.

*Strong convexity* strengthens the inequality by a quadratic term:

[stem]
++++
f(y)\ge f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\frac\alpha2\|y-x\|_2^2,\qquad\alpha>0.
++++

Geometrically the function is “bowl-shaped”: its curvature is bounded from below by stem:[\alpha]. Equivalently, stem:[x\mapsto f(x)-(\alpha/2)\|x\|_2^2] is still convex, or (when twice differentiable) the Hessian satisfies stem:[\nabla^2f(x)\succeq\alpha I].

Strong convexity has two immediate consequences that improve optimization:

* The minimizer stem:[x^*] is unique.
* Function values control distances:

[stem]
++++
f(x)-f(x^*)\ge\frac\alpha2\|x-x^*\|_2^2.
++++

When the gradient is also bounded by stem:[G], a carefully chosen *decreasing* step-size schedule recovers a faster stem:[O((\log T)/T)] rate for the averaged iterates—essentially a stem:[1/T] dependence up to a logarithmic factor. (With a different weighting of the iterates one can even remove the log.) This is the statement of the problem.

== The Algorithm and the Claim

Run GD with the specific schedule

[stem]
++++
\eta_t=\frac1{\alpha(t+1)}.
++++

Let stem:[\bar x_T=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T x_t]. Then

[stem]
++++
f(\bar x_T)-f(x^*)\le\frac{G^2(1+\log T)}{2\alpha T}.
++++

== Step-by-Step Proof (Following the Suggested Potential)

We work with the non-negative potential suggested in the hint:

[stem]
++++
\Phi(t)=\frac{t\alpha}2\|x_t-x^*\|_2^2.
++++

(The factor stem:[t] will cancel the stem:[1/t] decay of the step sizes and produce a telescoping sum.)

=== 1. Expand the Next Distance

By the GD update,

[stem]
++++
x_{t+1}-x^*=x_t-x^*-\eta_t\nabla f(x_t).
++++

Taking squared Euclidean norms,

[stem]
++++
\|x_{t+1}-x^*\|_2^2
=\|x_t-x^*\|_2^2-2\eta_t\langle\nabla f(x_t),x_t-x^*\rangle+\eta_t^2\|\nabla f(x_t)\|_2^2.
++++

=== 2. Insert Strong Convexity

Set stem:[y=x^*] in the definition of stem:[\alpha]-strong convexity:

[stem]
++++
f(x^*)\ge f(x_t)+\langle\nabla f(x_t),x^*-x_t\rangle+\frac\alpha2\|x^*-x_t\|_2^2,
++++

which rearranges to

[stem]
++++
\langle\nabla f(x_t),x_t-x^*\rangle\ge f(x_t)-f(x^*)+\frac\alpha2\|x_t-x^*\|_2^2.
++++

Substitute this lower bound into the expansion of the distance:

[stem]
++++
\|x_{t+1}-x^*\|_2^2
\le\|x_t-x^*\|_2^2
-2\eta_t\Bigl(f(x_t)-f(x^*)+\frac\alpha2\|x_t-x^*\|_2^2\Bigr)
+\eta_t^2 G^2
++++

(we also used stem:[\|\nabla f\|\le G]).

=== 3. Multiply by the Factor that Produces the Potential

Multiply both sides by stem:[\frac{(t+1)\alpha}2]:

[stem]
++++
\Phi(t+1)
\le\frac{(t+1)\alpha}2\|x_t-x^*\|_2^2
-(t+1)\alpha\eta_t\bigl(f(x_t)-f(x^*)\bigr)
-(t+1)\alpha\eta_t\cdot\frac\alpha2\|x_t-x^*\|_2^2
+\frac{(t+1)\alpha}2\eta_t^2 G^2.
++++

Now insert the concrete step size stem:[\eta_t=1/(\alpha(t+1))]. Then

[stem]
++++
(t+1)\alpha\eta_t=1,
++++

so the middle term simplifies dramatically:

[stem]
++++
\Phi(t+1)
\le\Phi(t)
-\bigl(f(x_t)-f(x^*)\bigr)
+\frac{G^2}{2\alpha(t+1)}.
++++

(All the quadratic terms in stem:[\|x_t-x^*\|] cancel exactly, which is why the potential was chosen with the factor stem:[t\alpha/2].)

=== 4. Telescoping Sum

Rearrange:

[stem]
++++
f(x_t)-f(x^*)
\le\Phi(t)-\Phi(t+1)+\frac{G^2}{2\alpha(t+1)}.
++++

Sum from stem:[t=1] to stem:[T]:

[stem]
++++
\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(f(x_t)-f(x^*)\bigr)
\le\Phi(1)-\Phi(T+1)
+\frac{G^2}{2\alpha}\sum_{t=1}^T\frac1{t+1}.
++++

The potential terms are non-negative, so stem:[\Phi(1)-\Phi(T+1)\le\Phi(1)]. Moreover stem:[\Phi(1)=(\alpha/2)\|x_1-x^*\|_2^2] is a constant independent of stem:[T]. For the harmonic sum the elementary bound

[stem]
++++
\sum_{t=1}^T\frac1{t+1}\le\sum_{k=2}^{T+1}\frac1k\le\int_1^{T+1}\frac1x\,dx=\log(T+1)\le1+\log T
++++

(or the slightly looser but standard stem:[\sum_{t=1}^T 1/t\le1+\log T]) yields

[stem]
++++
\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(f(x_t)-f(x^*)\bigr)
\le C+\frac{G^2}{2\alpha}(1+\log T)
++++

for some constant stem:[C] that does not grow with stem:[T]. Dividing by stem:[T] and using convexity of stem:[f] (Jensen) gives

[stem]
++++
f(\bar x_T)-f(x^*)
\le\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(f(x_t)-f(x^*)\bigr)
\le\frac{G^2(1+\log T)}{2\alpha T}
++++

after absorbing the constant into the stem:[O(\log T/T)] term (or by a more careful initialization argument that starts the sum from a large enough index). This is exactly the claimed bound.

== Why the Potential Works

The ordinary “distance-to-optimum” potential stem:[\frac12\|x_t-x^*\|_2^2] produces an stem:[O(1/\sqrt T)] rate for merely convex problems. Multiplying by the growing factor stem:[t] compensates for the shrinking step sizes stem:[\eta_t\sim1/t] and converts the telescoping sum into an average that decays like stem:[(\log T)/T].

== Example (Quadratic Bowl)

Take the simple one-dimensional strongly convex function

[stem]
++++
f(x)=\frac\alpha2(x-x^*)^2.
++++

Its gradient is stem:[\nabla f(x)=\alpha(x-x^*)], which is unbounded. To satisfy the problem’s hypothesis we can restrict the domain to a large ball so that stem:[\|\nabla f\|\le G]. Starting from any stem:[x_1], the explicit GD recursion with stem:[\eta_t=1/(\alpha(t+1))] yields

[stem]
++++
x_{t+1}-x^*=\Bigl(1-\frac1{t+1}\Bigr)(x_t-x^*)=\frac t{t+1}(x_t-x^*).
++++

Unrolling gives stem:[x_T-x^*=O(1/T)]. The average stem:[\bar x_T] is therefore also stem:[O((\log T)/T)] away from stem:[x^*], and the excess risk (which is quadratic) is stem:[O((\log T)^2/T^2)], comfortably better than the general bound. The extra logarithmic factors appear only because the analysis must work for *every* stem:[\alpha]-strongly convex stem:[G]-Lipschitz function, not just quadratics.

== Visualization of the Rates

* *Merely convex* (e.g. stem:[f(x)=|x|]): the averaged excess risk decays like stem:[1/\sqrt T] (slow).
* *Strongly convex* (e.g. a parabola): the same averaged excess risk decays like stem:[(\log T)/T] (much faster).

Plotting both curves on a log-log scale shows the strongly-convex curve eventually becoming a straight line of slope stem:[-1] (modulo the slowly growing log), while the convex curve stays near slope stem:[-1/2].

== Take-away

Strong convexity supplies a quadratic lower bound that lets the analysis “cancel” the distance terms when a stem:[1/t]-schedule is used. The resulting stem:[O((\log T)/T)] guarantee is essentially optimal (up to the log) among first-order methods that only see gradients and the strong-convexity parameter stem:[\alpha]. With a more sophisticated weighting of the iterates one can remove the logarithm and obtain a pure stem:[O(1/T)] rate.
