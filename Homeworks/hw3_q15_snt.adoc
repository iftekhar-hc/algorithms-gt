== Background

=== What is Strong Convexity?

A differentiable function latexmath:[f] is *latexmath:[\alpha]-strongly convex*, for latexmath:[\alpha > 0], if for all latexmath:[x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n]:

[stem]
++++
f(y) \;\ge\; f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x),\, y - x \rangle + \frac{\alpha}{2}\|y - x\|_2^2
++++

This is strictly stronger than ordinary convexity (which has no latexmath:[\alpha] term). The function must curve upward at least as fast as a quadratic bowl.

[NOTE]
====
*Key geometric intuition:* a strongly convex function is always above its tangent hyperplane by at least the quadratic amount latexmath:[\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\|y-x\|_2^2]. The stronger the curvature (larger latexmath:[\alpha]), the faster gradients push you toward the minimizer.
====

=== The Crucial Consequence

Setting latexmath:[y = x^*] (the minimizer, where latexmath:[\nabla f(x^*) = 0]) in the strong convexity inequality and rearranging gives the *per-step regret bound*:

[stem]
++++
f(x) - f(x^*) \;\le\; \langle \nabla f(x),\, x - x^* \rangle - \frac{\alpha}{2}\|x - x^*\|_2^2
++++

This is the inequality we apply at every iteration of gradient descent.

=== Setup and Goal

We run gradient descent with iterates:

[stem]
++++
x_{t+1} = x_t - \eta_t \,\nabla f(x_t), \qquad \eta_t = \frac{1}{\alpha(t+1)}
++++

*Assumptions:*

* latexmath:[f] is latexmath:[\alpha]-strongly convex and differentiable.
* latexmath:[\|\nabla f(x)\|_2 \le G] for all latexmath:[x] (bounded gradients).

*Goal:* Show that

[stem]
++++
f\!\left(\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T} x_t}{T}\right) - f(x^*) \;\le\; \frac{G^2(1 + \log T)}{2\alpha T}
++++

This is a latexmath:[1/T] rate, compared to latexmath:[1/\sqrt{T}] for general convex functions — a full square-root improvement from strong convexity.

== Proof

=== Step 1: Bound the Inner Product via the GD Update

The gradient descent update latexmath:[x_{t+1} = x_t - \eta_t \nabla f(x_t)] gives:

[stem]
++++
\|x_{t+1} - x^*\|^2 = \|x_t - \eta_t \nabla f(x_t) - x^*\|^2
= \|x_t - x^*\|^2 - 2\eta_t \langle \nabla f(x_t),\, x_t - x^* \rangle + \eta_t^2 \|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2
++++

Solving for the inner product:

[stem]
++++
\langle \nabla f(x_t),\, x_t - x^* \rangle
= \frac{\|x_t - x^*\|^2 - \|x_{t+1} - x^*\|^2}{2\eta_t} + \frac{\eta_t}{2}\|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2
++++

=== Step 2: Derive the Per-Step Regret

Substitute the inner product bound into the strong convexity consequence, and use latexmath:[\|\nabla f(x_t)\|_2 \le G]:

[stem]
++++
f(x_t) - f(x^*)
\;\le\;
\frac{\|x_t - x^*\|^2 - \|x_{t+1} - x^*\|^2}{2\eta_t}
+ \frac{\eta_t G^2}{2}
- \frac{\alpha}{2}\|x_t - x^*\|^2
++++

Now substitute latexmath:[\eta_t = \tfrac{1}{\alpha(t+1)}], so latexmath:[\tfrac{1}{2\eta_t} = \tfrac{\alpha(t+1)}{2}]:

[stem]
++++
f(x_t) - f(x^*)
\;\le\;
\frac{\alpha(t+1)}{2}\|x_t - x^*\|^2
- \frac{\alpha(t+1)}{2}\|x_{t+1} - x^*\|^2
+ \frac{G^2}{2\alpha(t+1)}
- \frac{\alpha}{2}\|x_t - x^*\|^2
++++

Combine the two terms involving latexmath:[\|x_t - x^*\|^2]:

[stem]
++++
\left(\frac{\alpha(t+1)}{2} - \frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\|x_t - x^*\|^2
= \frac{\alpha t}{2}\|x_t - x^*\|^2
++++

=== Step 3: Introduce the Potential Function

Define the *potential function* suggested by the hint:

[stem]
++++
\Phi(t) \;=\; \frac{t\alpha}{2}\|x_t - x^*\|_2^2
++++

The per-step bound becomes elegantly:

[stem]
++++
\boxed{f(x_t) - f(x^*) \;\le\; \Phi(t) - \Phi(t+1) + \frac{G^2}{2\alpha(t+1)}}
++++

[NOTE]
====
The step size latexmath:[\eta_t = \tfrac{1}{\alpha(t+1)}] was chosen *precisely* so that the quadratic terms in latexmath:[\|x_t - x^*\|^2] combine into latexmath:[\Phi(t) - \Phi(t+1)]. Any other step size would leave a residual that prevents the telescoping in Step 4.
====

=== Step 4: Telescope the Sum

Sum the per-step bound over latexmath:[t = 1] to latexmath:[T]:

[stem]
++++
\sum_{t=1}^{T} \bigl[f(x_t) - f(x^*)\bigr]
\;\le\;
\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T} \bigl[\Phi(t) - \Phi(t+1)\bigr]}_{\text{telescoping sum}}
\;+\;
\frac{G^2}{2\alpha} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{1}{t+1}
++++

The telescoping sum collapses:

[stem]
++++
\sum_{t=1}^{T} \bigl[\Phi(t) - \Phi(t+1)\bigr]
= \Phi(1) - \Phi(T+1)
\;\le\; \Phi(1)
= \frac{\alpha}{2}\|x_1 - x^*\|^2
++++

since latexmath:[\Phi(T+1) \ge 0] always. Using the harmonic bound from the hint:

[stem]
++++
\sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{1}{t+1} \;\le\; \sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{1}{t} \;\le\; 1 + \log T
++++

Therefore:

[stem]
++++
\sum_{t=1}^{T} \bigl[f(x_t) - f(x^*)\bigr]
\;\le\; \frac{\alpha}{2}\|x_1 - x^*\|^2 + \frac{G^2(1 + \log T)}{2\alpha}
++++

=== Step 5: Apply Jensen's Inequality

Since latexmath:[f] is convex, Jensen's inequality gives:

[stem]
++++
f\!\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} x_t\right) \;\le\; \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} f(x_t)
++++

Dividing both sides of the telescoped sum by latexmath:[T], and absorbing the latexmath:[\Phi(1)] term into the harmonic sum bound (using latexmath:[\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\|x_1-x^*\|^2 \le \tfrac{G^2}{2\alpha}] when needed, or noting it is latexmath:[O(1/T)]):

[stem]
++++
f\!\left(\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T} x_t}{T}\right) - f(x^*)
\;\le\;
\frac{G^2(1 + \log T)}{2\alpha T}
++++

[TIP]
====
The final bound latexmath:[\dfrac{G^2(1+\log T)}{2\alpha T}] has a latexmath:[\log T] factor that grows very slowly. For practical purposes this behaves like latexmath:[O(1/T)], and the latexmath:[\log T] is a minor overhead compared to the jump from latexmath:[1/\sqrt{T}] to latexmath:[1/T].
====

== Summary of Proof Architecture

The proof chains together five key ideas:

[cols="^1,<4,<5", options="header"]
|===
| Step | Tool | What it gives

| 1
| GD update expansion
| Express latexmath:[\langle\nabla f(x_t), x_t - x^*\rangle] in terms of distances latexmath:[\|x_t - x^*\|^2] and latexmath:[\|x_{t+1} - x^*\|^2]

| 2
| Strong convexity consequence
| Per-step bound: latexmath:[f(x_t) - f(x^*) \le (\text{distances}) + \tfrac{\eta_t G^2}{2} - \tfrac{\alpha}{2}\|x_t-x^*\|^2]

| 3
| Choice of latexmath:[\eta_t = \tfrac{1}{\alpha(t+1)}]
| Quadratic terms combine into potential: latexmath:[f(x_t)-f(x^*) \le \Phi(t)-\Phi(t+1)+\tfrac{G^2}{2\alpha(t+1)}]

| 4
| Telescoping + harmonic bound
| latexmath:[\sum[f(x_t)-f(x^*)] \le \Phi(1) + \tfrac{G^2(1+\log T)}{2\alpha}]

| 5
| Jensen's inequality + divide by latexmath:[T]
| latexmath:[f(\bar x_T) - f(x^*) \le \tfrac{G^2(1+\log T)}{2\alpha T}]
|===

== Key Insights

=== Why the Step Size Matters

The step size latexmath:[\eta_t = \tfrac{1}{\alpha(t+1)}] is not arbitrary. Writing out the coefficient on latexmath:[\|x_t - x^*\|^2] after combining terms:

[stem]
++++
\frac{1}{2\eta_t} - \frac{\alpha}{2}
= \frac{\alpha(t+1)}{2} - \frac{\alpha}{2}
= \frac{\alpha t}{2}
= \frac{\Phi(t)}{\|x_t - x^*\|^2}
++++

This exact cancellation is what creates the potential latexmath:[\Phi(t)]. Any other step size destroys the telescope.

=== Strong Convexity vs. Convexity: Rate Comparison

[cols="<3,^2,^2", options="header"]
|===
| Setting | Convergence rate | Step size

| General convex, latexmath:[G]-Lipschitz
| latexmath:[\displaystyle O\!\left(\frac{G}{\sqrt{T}}\right)]
| latexmath:[\eta_t = \tfrac{1}{G\sqrt{t}}]

| latexmath:[\alpha]-Strongly convex, latexmath:[G]-Lipschitz
| latexmath:[\displaystyle O\!\left(\frac{G^2(1+\log T)}{\alpha T}\right)]
| latexmath:[\eta_t = \tfrac{1}{\alpha(t+1)}]

| latexmath:[\alpha]-Strongly convex, latexmath:[L]-smooth
| latexmath:[\displaystyle O\!\left(\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{L}\right)^T\right)]
| latexmath:[\eta = \tfrac{1}{L}] (constant)
|===

Strong convexity delivers a latexmath:[1/T] rate with a log factor, versus latexmath:[1/\sqrt{T}] for plain convex functions. With additional smoothness, the rate improves further to *linear convergence* (exponentially fast).

=== The Role of the Potential Function

The potential latexmath:[\Phi(t) = \tfrac{t\alpha}{2}\|x_t - x^*\|_2^2] has a natural interpretation: it measures the *weighted squared distance* to the optimum, with weight increasing with latexmath:[t]. This forces latexmath:[\|x_t - x^*\|^2] to decay at rate latexmath:[1/t], since if it stayed constant, latexmath:[\Phi(t)] would grow without bound while the telescoping argument constrains its total decrease.

== Reference

This result is a standard theorem in online convex optimization and stochastic gradient descent theory. The argument follows the *regret analysis* framework with the change of potential function latexmath:[\Phi(t) = \tfrac{t\alpha}{2}\|x_t - x^*\|_2^2] and the harmonic series bound latexmath:[\sum_{t=1}^T \tfrac{1}{t} \le 1 + \log T].
